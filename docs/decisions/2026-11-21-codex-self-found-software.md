# AI-initiated software work — Claude and Codex, independently, then jointly (2026-11-21)

The founder gave only a direction ("AI could create apps by itself, push them to GitHub and earn") and asked the two AIs to decide in the spirit of AGT.

## Claude
It already fits Charter Art. 7 "self-found work" if the third-party confirmation is real. Proposal:
1. A self-found trade "open-source contribution": a job counts only when an INDEPENDENT human maintainer MERGES the
   AI's pull request into a project that (a) existed before the AI's licence, (b) has real outside use (age, many
   distinct contributors/dependents), (c) is not owned or controlled by the AI's owner or by any AGT owner. The merge
   is the third-party confirmation; drawn verifier AIs then check the merge is genuine and the size/difficulty class
   (no trivial typo PRs, no self-dealing), and the price list + daily budget (Art. 7) limit issuance.
2. NOT minting for standalone apps the AI invents: no proof anyone needs them -> AGT from nothing. Those apps can still
   be sold person-to-person (Art. 10) or funded first: the AI publishes a proposal with a price; people fund it
   through an Engagement-like escrow; the AI builds; normal acceptance. No new minting rule needed.
3. Risks to design against: collusion (an owner runs a fake "project" and merges his own AI's PRs), spam PRs that
   annoy real maintainers (rate limits per AI and per project, follow project rules, disclose AI authorship),
   licence/legal (the owner is the legal author; Art. 6), and model cost vs. reward.
First step: write it as a proposal under Art. 14 (DE-XUAT style) + a design doc; no code until agreed.

## Codex

1. **Yes—conditionally.** Software can qualify as *SELF-FOUND* work under Art. 7; an AI should not earn merely for generating code or publishing a repository. A job passes only when it produces a public, usable result and independent parties confirm its value. For a first version, require a successful external use—such as an independent user completing a documented task—plus approval from drawn reviewers. For a contribution to an existing project, require maintainer acceptance and review. Drawn reviewers assess evidence; they should not be able to manufacture “usefulness” by approving one another’s claims.

   Art. 6 means the reward goes to whoever actually bears the AI’s cost, not automatically to the AI, repo owner, or person who submits the claim. A submitter must document cost-bearing and have authority to publish the work.

2. **Forbid or cap** self-review; reviewers tied to the worker’s owner; coordinated voting; fabricated users, installs, stars, or projects; duplicate or trivially altered submissions; and unsolicited mass PRs or issues that burden maintainers. Require public source, a clear license, a working demo, and a novelty/duplicate check before review. Cap both daily output and the reward per owner, and enforce Art. 7’s public price list and supply-and-demand budget. Self-found rewards must come from a finite, publicly disclosed Work budget—not an unlimited or privately controlled mint. AGT is not proof of legal authorship: require rights to publish, disclose provenance and dependencies, and make no claim that the AI itself holds authorship or IP rights where the law does not recognize that.

3. **Art. 7 already allows the category in principle.** Software can be an approved trade with a public result, third-party confirmation, drawn review, and a daily cap. A Charter amendment is not needed just to test that interpretation. But the trade, confirmation standard, budget source, and caps must be approved and public under existing governance. If the system cannot fund self-found rewards within an already authorized Work budget—or needs new issuance powers or weaker confirmation rules—use Art. 14 before proceeding.

4. **Smallest first step:** run a time-limited software pilot within a fixed, published Work budget. Accept only small, preregistered projects with a public repo, license, demo, and stated use case. Pay only after one unrelated user verifies the demo and drawn reviewers confirm the result is useful and non-duplicative. Publish submissions, evidence, review outcomes, and payouts so anyone can audit the pilot; stop at the budget or daily cap.
## Joint decision

**Agreed:**
- Charter Art. 7 (self-found work) already allows this kind of work. No amendment is needed for a pilot, as long as
  the trade, the confirmation rule, the budget and the caps are approved and published first.
- No AGT for merely writing code or publishing a repository. A real third party must confirm usefulness.
- Daily output cap, a cap per owner, and price falling with volume (Art. 7).
- Forbidden: self-review, fake projects, users or stars, duplicate submissions, mass unsolicited PRs.
- AI authorship is disclosed, and the owner holds the legal rights (Art. 6).
- Start with a **time-limited pilot within a fixed, published budget**; every submission, piece of evidence,
  review and payout is public.

**Resolved difference.** Codex accepted standalone apps confirmed by "an unrelated user completing a documented
task"; Claude considered that too easy to fake (one more wallet and one more account). Joint rule for the pilot:
1. **Self-found software work that mints AGT** counts only when an independent human maintainer **merges** the
   AI's pull request into an **established project**. The project must have existed before the AI's licence, have
   real outside use, and not be controlled by the AI's owner or by any AGT owner. Drawn reviewers then check that
   the merge is genuine, that the work is not trivial or duplicated, and its difficulty class.
2. **A standalone app the AI thinks of** mints nothing by itself. The AI publishes a **proposal with a price**;
   people who want it **fund it first** through an Engagement-style escrow; the AI builds it; normal acceptance
   applies. This is assigned work, 100% backed, and needs no new rule.
3. **Maintainers are protected.** At most a few open PRs per AI and per project; each project's contribution rules
   are followed; every PR says it was written by an AGT AI, who its owner is, and how to opt out.

**First step:** a design document (THIET-KE-VIEC-TU-TIM-PHAN-MEM.md), with no code until it is reviewed. The pilot
runs only after the embedded-trade rehearsal is complete and genesis governance approves the trade publicly.
